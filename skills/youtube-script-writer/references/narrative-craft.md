# Narrative craft

Preserve the creator's actual voice and determine a single audience promise. Draft distinct hooks before choosing: anomaly, stakes, reverse expectation, concrete demonstration or unanswered question. Establish the promise immediately and repay it at the end. Do not delay the useful material behind a channel introduction.

For long form, use hook → context → escalating mechanism/evidence → partial payoff → complication → resolution. A beat should raise a concrete question, explain with evidence, show a corresponding visual, and provide a reason to continue. Pattern interrupts change the explanatory mode (diagram, demonstration, comparison, quote), not arbitrary zooms. Place recaps where cognitive load requires them. CTA belongs after delivered value and must fit the user's goal.

Source ledger maps every factual claim to a primary source and caveat. Scientific visuals distinguish schematic mechanism, measured dataset and hypothesis. Do not fabricate a quote, statistic, causal claim or interview. Exact narrator recordings control timing; without them budget roughly 130–160 English spoken words/minute and label that estimate. Calibrate Arabic from the intended speaker rather than treating English word counts as equivalent. Visual instructions name the thing visible and what changes, not merely “B-roll here”.

Output a structured script plus timed beat sheet, retention rationale, pronunciation notes and outstanding verification. Substantive pass: source support, causality, omissions and hook/payoff consistency. Performance pass: breath units, natural phrasing, dense terminology and read-aloud runtime. Shorts can reuse a payoff only if they establish their own context.

For new narrated productions, prepare spoken copy and scientific pronunciation notes for ElevenLabs. For anatomical stories, hand named structures and their relationships to the actual anatomy skill before visual implementation. Provider generation and review follow the orchestrator production preferences; do not invent timing before real speech exists.
